2D Kinematics: Ballistic Trajectories Under Quadratic Air Resistance

Author: Lead Researcher Series: Grade 12 Advanced Dynamics Status: ● Verified by 18 Proofs

1. Abstract & Problem Formulation

In classical Newtonian mechanics, projectile motion in a vacuum follows a perfectly symmetric parabolic curve with range maximized at launch angle $\theta = 45^\circ$. However, in planetary atmospheres, the medium exerts an opposing drag force $F_d = -k v^2$, converting kinetic energy into thermal dissipation. This study analyzes the closed-form differential equations governing this trajectory distortion.

2. Mathematical Equations of Motion

Ideal Vacuum Parabola:
$$R_{\text{vac}} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}, \quad y(x) = x\tan\theta - \frac{g x^2}{2 v_0^2 \cos^2\theta}$$
Atmospheric Drag Vector Differential Form:
$$m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = m\vec{g} - k \|\vec{v}\| \vec{v} \implies \begin{cases} \frac{dv_x}{dt} = -k v v_x \\ \frac{dv_y}{dt} = -g - k v v_y \end{cases}$$

I. Fundamental Derivations

Under constant gravitational acceleration $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$, the equations of motion decouple horizontally and vertically:

$R_{\text{vacuum}} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}, \quad H_{\max} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2(\theta)}{2g}$

II. Non-Linear Atmosphere Perturbation

With drag parameter $k = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_d A / m$, the horizontal speed decays exponentially at initial onset and transitions into a velocity-squared loss. At $v_0 = 45\,\text{m/s}$, maximum range drops by $30.3\%$ ($206.4\,\text{m} \to 143.8\,\text{m}$).
